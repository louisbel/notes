AI Agents & Marketing Productivity

The sharp increases in marketing productivity ahead of us might not benefit all equally

May 5, 2025 - 3 min

Early deployments of AI agents on performance marketing workflows are showing significant promises. In the next two years, there is an opportunity for marketers to acquire new skills and leapfrog competitors.

Looking back at distinct phases of accelerated productivity gains in my career, four pivotal periods stand out:

  1. Foundational Technical Agility - These are skills acquired early on, often process related and taught by others directly on the job. Things like Excel shortcuts, Pivot Charts or VBA macros. Prior to the black box era of fully automated campaigns, these skills would deliver a significant competitive advantage by way of the granular insights they help uncover and their impact on marketing performance at scale.

  2. No-Code Automation - As cloud applications started going mainstream (Supermetrics, Zapier), technical marketers gained leverage through workflow automation tools, being able to connect APIs across the digital stack and automate reporting workflows with little to no coding experience.

  3. LLM Enhanced Coding - With LLMs, complex data visualizations and manipulations that once required advanced coding knowledge have become accessible through skilled prompting and AI assisted debugging. The one reporting feature that’s always been missing from Meta or Google Ads but was too expensive to build internally is only a couple of hours of tinkering away.

  4. Marketing Agents - New breeds of SAAS tools are empowering technical marketers to set reasoning chains that can both think & act based on a set of inputs. This might not seem like a big deal until one realizes a majority of the day to day of Marketing operations in non leadership roles consists in either making tactical decisions based on a set of inputs or producing deliverables and insights based on such inputs.

As marketing productivity accelerates both in pace and impact, we face two complementary possibilities:

➡️ A massive workforce knowledge gap: Entry-level marketers missing exposure to the “grunt work” might struggle to understand ecosystem boundaries, recognize diminishing returns, gauge task complexity, and ultimately fail to build the personal knowledge base that is the source of inspiration to deploy new AI use cases. Deloitte has produced an interesting path for executives to mitigate this issue.1

➡️ A Winner-Takes-All scenario: Technical Marketers who can engineer useful workflows connecting data to reasoning chains that produce both insights and solutions will gain exponential productivity leverage. Not only is a single person delivering faster on their own, but their output is simply not replicable by a larger group of peers using traditional workflows.

Unless, as one researcher hypothesized in 19782, widespread LLM access leads to an unfortunate regression to the mean, equalizing competitive advantages (same creative tactics, same user journeys everywhere). A hopeful view for creative minds!
